The mind of a child is a wonderful thing. In [a previous post](/writing/the-lego-set-that-builds-itself), I wrote about the parts of AI that drain me and make me a little sad. This one is about what I love.

The other day, my six-year-old came to me and said, "Dad, we should make a game called Cactus Boats." I loved it. I asked him a few questions about how it should work, handed him a piece of paper, and he drew the art. We were off to the races.

You captain a tiny pirate ship with a cactus for a mast and sink the patrols that keep coming, until they sink you. Fire your cannons and see how far up the leaderboard you can get.

Before AI, a game like this would have taken me days, and I probably never would have gotten it hosted anywhere I could share it. This time it took a few sessions, and now we have a working game we can play together. It’s been a blast for him to see his idea come to life that fast, and a blast for me to build something with him.

This is the part of AI I’m not conflicted about.
