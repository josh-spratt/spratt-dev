“Why do I never finish projects?”

I sat down and did a bit of soul searching and looked at what I’d built over the last few months:

- `twit.rs`, a Twitter clone written in Rust
- `spill`, a web app for family interaction (where you spill your guts anonymously about a fun/silly topic each day)
- `doorchime`, an AOL Instant Messenger clone
- A historically accurate Titanic sinking simulation game

They were all fun to build. Every single one of them is dead now. I built them, hosted them, and then could not get anyone to try them out. But here’s the thing: I did finish them. Every one of them shipped.

If you read [my last post](/writing/the-lego-set-that-builds-itself), you know I struggle to get the same fulfillment out of building with AI. But AI is the reason most of these projects got finished at all. Back in the old days, they would have died a half-baked repo on my GitHub profile. These projects still got built. We started with an idea, and shipped a real working version of that idea. That counts for something.

Building used to be the hard part. It was also the fun part. Now, building is fast and easy. Most of the fun is gone. Finding the right thing to build is the new hard part.

I loved building the Titanic game. I loved playing it too. I’m a huge history and shipwrecks nerd. But you can only play your own game so many times. If I can’t get anyone else interested in it, what’s the point? I used to feel rewarded twice: the work, and then the people using it. AI took the first. Nobody showed up for the second.

So, where does this leave me? What were my takeaways?

1. Building something and then tearing it down is not a failure. You still had a fun idea and brought it to life. That’s worth being proud of.
2. The hard part has shifted (or maybe always was) to identifying the right thing to build next, and how to get anyone to care. I want to get better at that, and find a way to enjoy it as much as I used to enjoy the building.

Everyone has a project graveyard. What’s in yours?
