Engineering has never felt more isolating.

Think back to what first got you into this field. For a lot of us, it was being a bright-eyed kid building something out of a pile of Lego bricks. That feeling lit a fire, a passion to build.

Landing in a career where you got paid to build things and solve hard problems was wonderful. That’s exactly why adapting to AI is hard for some of us. Are we still building? Of course. But is a Lego set that builds itself as fun as one you designed and assembled by hand? No.

Add the pressure from executives, and even tech leaders, to go all in on AI. (“Why aren’t you a 10x developer yet?”) And God forbid your algorithm picks up on any of this on X or TikTok. You’ll be living in AI-psychosis doom and gloom for the rest of your days.

Here’s the isolating part. Most of the people around me seem unfazed. I felt like I was just getting started, like I was finally becoming a good engineer. Now it feels like that doesn’t matter anymore. And when I try to explain this to leaders, they just don’t get it.

Don’t get me wrong. I use these tools every day. I even used them to build this site. If we’re not growing, we’re dying, and I know this is where I need to grow. It’s just not where my heart is.

So why am I writing these posts?

- To flex the ol’ brain muscle and see if I can still string a coherent sentence together.
- Because a lot of engineers feel alone and out in the cold right now. You’re not alone! If even one person reads these and feels a little less isolated, it’s worth it.
- Because it’s fun to write something and throw it up on the public internet!

So follow along. I’ll keep posting here, along with whatever I’m building. You can grab the [RSS feed](/writing/rss.xml) or just check back now and then.
